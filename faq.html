Connecticut Youth Soccer FAQ

Real answers to the questions parents and players search for most — league acronyms, how tryouts work, age groups, cost, and how to find a club near you. Some answers depend heavily on the specific club or program, so we've noted where to confirm directly rather than assume a single statewide rule.

What's the difference between CJSA, EDP, ECNL, and MLS Next?
CJSA (Connecticut Junior Soccer Association) is the state's governing/membership body — the framework most organized CT youth clubs register through for player registration, background-checked coaching, insurance, and State Cup play. EDP (Elite Development Program) and ECNL (Elite Clubs National League) are competitive league platforms a club's travel teams can play in — EDP is a respected regional Northeast league, ECNL one of the top national platforms for elite competitive soccer. MLS Next is MLS's own national development pathway for boys, tied to pro club academies (Girls Academy is the girls'-side equivalent, tied to NWSL academies). A club can be a CJSA member and compete in EDP, ECNL, or MLS Next at the same time — CJSA answers "who governs registration," the others answer "who do we play against."
What age groups do Connecticut travel and competitive clubs typically offer?
Most CT travel and competitive clubs run age groups from about U6/U7 through U18/U19, often with an even younger recreational entry point — some clubs start intro programs as early as 18 months to U5 — before travel soccer typically begins around U8–U9.
What's the difference between recreational, travel, and premier/elite soccer?
Recreational soccer is entry-level and open to all skill levels, usually organized by town with no tryouts. Travel soccer is tryout-based and competitive, with teams playing other towns and clubs regionally. Premier/elite soccer (top EDP/State Cup tiers, ECNL, MLS Next, Girls Academy) is the highest competitive level, often involving regional or national travel, showcases, and more visibility for college recruiting.
How do youth soccer tryouts work in Connecticut, and when are they usually held?
Most CT travel and competitive clubs hold tryouts in late spring or early summer for the following fall season, sometimes with a smaller second window in early fall for open roster spots. Players typically attend one or two evaluation sessions run by club coaches, with roster offers going out within a couple of weeks. Exact dates and formats vary by club — check the specific club's own tryout info on its CT Soccer profile rather than assuming a single statewide schedule.
What is CJSA, and how is it different from an individual club?
CJSA (Connecticut Junior Soccer Association) is the state affiliate of US Youth Soccer — the sanctioning and membership organization most organized CT youth clubs register through. CJSA itself doesn't run teams or hold tryouts; an individual club is the organization that actually fields teams, runs tryouts, and delivers programs, typically operating as a CJSA member.
How do I find a soccer club near me in Connecticut?
Browse the Clubs by Town directory, which lists every tracked club grouped by the town it's based in, or use the Full Directory on the dashboard to search and filter by tier.
What's the difference between a youth club and a semi-pro/pro club like Hartford Athletic or CT United FC?
Youth clubs are development-focused organizations for players roughly age 3 through 19, built around training and player development rather than paid competition. Semi-pro and pro clubs field adult or college-age rosters competing for real results in a professional or semi-professional league pyramid — Hartford Athletic (USL Championship) and CT United FC (MLS Next Pro) are fully professional, while AC Connecticut and CT Rush (USL League Two) and New Haven United FC (NPSL) are semi-professional. Some, like CT United FC, also run their own youth academy underneath the first team as a separate pathway.
Do I need to live in a specific town to join a CT club?
Generally no for travel and competitive clubs — most draw players from a wide, multi-town catchment area, and several CT clubs explicitly serve multiple towns or counties. Some purely recreational, town-run leagues do restrict registration to town residents, though, so it's worth confirming directly with a specific program before assuming.
How can I watch Hartford Athletic, CT United FC, or other CT pro/semi-pro teams play?
Hartford Athletic (USL Championship) plays at Trinity Health Stadium in Hartford. CT United FC (MLS Next Pro) currently splits home matches between UConn's Morrone Stadium and Yale's Reese Stadium while its permanent Bridgeport stadium is under development. AC Connecticut, CT Rush (USL League Two), and New Haven United FC (NPSL) also play home matches in-state. Check each club's page for its current schedule — start from the Adult Leagues section.
What options exist for adults who want to play organized soccer in Connecticut?
AC Connecticut and CT Rush both compete in USL League Two, the top tier of college-eligible amateur soccer in the US, and are the main organized adult pathways CT Soccer tracks. Beyond that, many CJSA-affiliated clubs and independent leagues around the state run recreational adult divisions — those aren't centrally tracked here, so check with clubs or leagues local to you.
How much does youth club soccer cost in Connecticut?
It varies widely by club, competitive tier, and age group — recreational programs are often a few hundred dollars a year, while elite travel and academy programs can run into the thousands once tournament travel, uniforms, and coaching fees are included. Many clubs offer financial aid or scholarship programs, so it's worth asking directly when you contact a club rather than assuming cost rules it out.
